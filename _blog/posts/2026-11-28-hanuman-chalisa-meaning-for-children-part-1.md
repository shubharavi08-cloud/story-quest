## 1. What is the Hanuman Chalisa?

*Chalisa* comes from *chalis*, forty. The prayer has **forty chaupais** (four-part verses, sung as two lines each), framed by **dohas** (couplets) at the start and the end. It was written by the poet-saint **Goswami Tulsidas**, who also wrote the *Ramcharitmanas*, in **Awadhi**, a form of Hindi. That is why the words sound a little different from the Hindi children hear today.

Part 1 covers the **two opening dohas and chaupais 1 to 10**: the prayer for a clear mind, the names and looks of Hanuman, his qualities, and his great deeds in the Ramayana.

## 2. The opening dohas: cleaning the mirror of the mind

> श्रीगुरु चरन सरोज रज, निज मनु मुकुरु सुधारि।
> बरनउँ रघुबर बिमल जसु, जो दायकु फल चारि॥

**Meaning:** "With the dust of my Guru's lotus feet, I clean the mirror of my mind. Then I sing the pure glory of Rama, which gives the four fruits of life."

The **four fruits** are the four goals of life in Indian tradition: *dharma* (doing right), *artha* (a good living), *kama* (healthy wishes) and *moksha* (freedom). The picture to share with a child is the **mirror**: when a mirror is dusty you cannot see clearly, and when the mind is full of anger or worry it cannot think clearly either. The prayer begins by asking for a clean mind.

> बुद्धिहीन तनु जानिके, सुमिरौं पवन-कुमार।
> बल बुधि बिद्या देहु मोहिं, हरहु कलेस बिकार॥

**Meaning:** "Knowing that I have little wisdom, I remember Hanuman, son of the Wind. Give me **strength, wisdom and learning**, and take away my troubles and the faults of my mind."

This is a good line for a child before an exam or a hard day: it does not ask for a prize, it asks for *bal, buddhi, vidya*, the strength and understanding to do the work well.

## 3. Chaupais 1 to 4: who Hanuman is and how he looks

> जय हनुमान ज्ञान गुन सागर। जय कपीस तिहुँ लोक उजागर॥

**1.** Victory to Hanuman, an **ocean of knowledge and good qualities**. Victory to the lord of the vanaras, who lights up the three worlds.

> राम दूत अतुलित बल धामा। अंजनि-पुत्र पवनसुत नामा॥

**2.** You are **Rama's messenger**, a home of strength that cannot be measured. You are called the son of **Anjana** and **Pavanasuta**, son of the Wind god.

> महाबीर बिक्रम बजरंगी। कुमति निवार सुमति के संगी॥

**3.** Great hero, brave one, with a body as strong as a *vajra* (thunderbolt; *Bajrangi* comes from *vajra-anga*). You **drive away bad thinking** and stay beside good thinking.

> कंचन बरन बिराज सुबेसा। कानन कुंडल कुंचित केसा॥

**4.** Your body shines like **gold**, you are finely dressed, with earrings in your ears and curly hair.

Ask your child to draw Hanuman from chaupai 4 alone: golden colour, fine clothes, earrings, curly hair. It is a picture painted in words.

## 4. Chaupais 5 to 8: his qualities

> हाथ बज्र औ ध्वजा बिराजै। काँधे मूँज जनेऊ साजै॥

**5.** In your hands shine the **vajra** and a **flag**, and a sacred thread of *munja* grass sits on your shoulder.

> संकर सुवन केसरीनंदन। तेज प्रताप महा जग बंदन॥

**6.** You are counted as a form of **Shiva** and are the son of **Kesari**, the vanara chief. The whole world bows to your shining glory.

> बिद्यावान गुनी अति चातुर। राम काज करिबे को आतुर॥

**7.** You are learned, full of good qualities and very wise, and always **eager to do Rama's work**.

> प्रभु चरित्र सुनिबे को रसिया। राम लखन सीता मन बसिया॥

**8.** You **love to listen to the story of the Lord**, and Rama, Lakshmana and Sita live in your heart.

Chaupai 7 is worth a pause. Hanuman is the strongest of all, yet the poet first praises him for being **learned and wise**, and then for being **eager to help**. Strength comes third. That order is a quiet lesson for children.

## 5. Chaupais 9 and 10: three forms, three great deeds

> सूक्ष्म रूप धरि सियहिं दिखावा। बिकट रूप धरि लंक जरावा॥

**9.** Taking a **tiny form**, you appeared before Sita. Taking a **fearsome form**, you burnt Lanka.

> भीम रूप धरि असुर सँहारे। रामचंद्र के काज सँवारे॥

**10.** Taking a **mighty form**, you destroyed the asuras, and you completed the work of Ramachandra.

Each line is a story from the Ramayana, the first two from the *Sundara Kanda*. In Ashoka Vatika, Hanuman made himself small, sat in a tree and gently showed Sita Rama's ring, so that she would know he came as a friend. When Ravana's soldiers set his tail on fire, he grew huge and leapt from roof to roof, setting Lanka alight. And in the battle he fought the demon armies so that Rama's mission could be finished. The lesson children enjoy here: **be small when gentleness is needed and big when courage is needed**.

## 6. Why do many families pray to Hanuman on Saturday?

In many homes, especially in South India, **Saturday** (*Shanivara*) is a Hanuman day, and in North India **Tuesday** is too. Saturday is the day of **Shani**, and a popular story says that Hanuman once freed Shani from Ravana's prison; in return, Shani promised not to trouble those who remember Hanuman. So families light a lamp, visit a Hanuman temple or recite the Chalisa on Saturdays. Customs differ from family to family, so ask your elders how your home keeps the day.

## 7. Five easy ways to learn it with your child

- **One chaupai a day.** Ten days, and Part 1 is done.
- **Meaning first, then the words.** Tell the story of the line, then sing it.
- **Draw it.** Chaupais 4, 5 and 9 make wonderful drawings.
- **Sing along to the video** with the Kannada meaning on screen.
- **Say the doha before homework:** *bal, buddhi, vidya dehu mohi*.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who wrote the Hanuman Chalisa?

Goswami Tulsidas, the 16th-century poet-saint who also wrote the Ramcharitmanas. He wrote it in Awadhi, a form of Hindi.

### Why is it called "Chalisa"?

Because it has forty (*chalis*) chaupais, with dohas at the beginning and the end.
