In many homes in Karnataka, Friday begins a little differently. The front step is washed, a fresh rangoli is drawn, a lamp is lit a little earlier and someone hums a song to **Goddess Lakshmi**. ## 1. Who is Lakshmi?

Lakshmi is the goddess of **prosperity, beauty and good fortune**. In the stories, she rose from the ocean of milk when the devas and asuras churned it, holding a lotus, and she chose Lord Vishnu as her companion. She is usually shown sitting or standing on a pink lotus, with gold coins flowing from one hand and elephants pouring water over her.

Children sometimes think Lakshmi only means money. Our elders explain it in a much bigger way, as you will see below.

## 2. Why Friday?

In Kannada, Friday is **ಶುಕ್ರವಾರ (Shukravara)**, the day of Shukra, the planet Venus. In Indian tradition, Shukra is linked with beauty, comfort and plenty, the same blessings that Lakshmi gives. So, in many families across South India, Friday became **Lakshmi's day**: the day to clean the house, decorate the doorway, light the lamp and sing to her.

The biggest Friday of the year in Karnataka is **Varamahalakshmi Vratha**, kept on a Friday in the month of Shravana, when women decorate a kalasha as the goddess and pray for the whole family. But every ordinary Friday can be a small Lakshmi day at home.

## 3. Eight kinds of wealth: the Ashtalakshmi

The tradition speaks of **Ashtalakshmi**, eight forms of Lakshmi, each a different kind of wealth. Families name them in slightly different ways; a common list is:

| Form | The wealth she gives |
|---|---|
| Adi Lakshmi | The first, original Lakshmi |
| Dhana Lakshmi | Money and possessions |
| Dhanya Lakshmi | Grain and food |
| Gaja Lakshmi | Strength and plenty, shown with elephants |
| Santana Lakshmi | Children and family |
| Veera (Dhairya) Lakshmi | Courage |
| Vijaya Lakshmi | Success and victory |
| Vidya Lakshmi | Knowledge and learning |

Ask your child: *which of these do we already have at home?* Food on the plate, a family who loves them, school books, courage to try something new: all of these are Lakshmi too. That is a wonderful way to teach **gratitude**.

## 4. A short prayer to learn

The best-known prayer to Mahalakshmi is the **Mahalakshmi Ashtakam**, traditionally said to be the prayer of Indra, king of the devas. Its first verse is short enough for a child to learn:

> नमस्तेऽस्तु महामाये श्रीपीठे सुरपूजिते ।
> शङ्खचक्रगदाहस्ते महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥

*Namaste'stu mahāmāye śrīpīṭhe surapūjite,*
*śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-haste mahālakṣmi namo'stu te.*

**Meaning:** "Salutations to you, great Mahamaya, seated on the throne of Shri, worshipped by the devas. You who hold the conch, the discus and the mace, O Mahalakshmi, salutations to you."

Say it slowly, two words at a time, and let your child repeat it after the lamp is lit.

## 5. Lakshmi comes where there is care

There is a lovely belief in many homes: **Lakshmi comes to a house that is clean, kind and peaceful**, and leaves a house full of quarrels and waste. That is why the doorway is cleaned and decorated on Fridays, why elders say not to waste food, and why we are asked not to speak harshly in the evening when the lamp is lit.

For children, this turns into simple habits. Lakshmi is not something we only ask for; she is something we look after.

## Five Friday habits to start today

- **Draw a small rangoli** at the door, even with chalk. Let your child choose the colours.
- **Light the lamp together** and play *Manege Baa Mahalakshmi* or say the verse above.
- **Finish what is on the plate.** Food is Dhanya Lakshmi; wasting it is the opposite of welcoming her.
- **Share one thing:** a snack, a toy, or coins in a small box for someone who needs it.
- **Say thank you** for one of the eight kinds of wealth you have this week.

## Questions to talk about

- "Which of Lakshmi's eight kinds of wealth do you like most, and why?"
- "What can we do to make our home a place where Lakshmi would like to come?"
- "Is a person who is kind but has little money rich or poor?"

## FAQ

### Why do people pray to Lakshmi on Friday?

Friday is Shukravara, the day of Shukra (Venus), which tradition links with beauty and plenty. Because these are Lakshmi's blessings, many South Indian families keep Friday as her day, with cleaning, rangoli, a lamp and songs.

### What is Varamahalakshmi?

Varamahalakshmi Vratha is a festival kept on a Friday in the month of Shravana, especially in Karnataka and nearby states, when the goddess is worshipped in a decorated kalasha for the wellbeing of the family.

### What are the Ashtalakshmi?

The eight forms of Lakshmi, each standing for one kind of wealth: the original Lakshmi, money, grain, strength, children, courage, victory and knowledge. Lists vary a little between traditions.
