This month Karnataka celebrates **Kannada Rajyotsava**, and our song *ನನ್ನ ನುಡಿ ಕನ್ನಡ · Nanna Nudi Kannada* (above) sings of a language carried "from Pampa to Kuvempu". Those two names stand about a thousand years apart. In between lies one of the richest poetry traditions in India.

Here are six poets from the song, one sentence each that a child can remember, and one thing to try at home.

## 1. Pampa (ಪಂಪ), the first great poet

Pampa lived in the **10th century** and is called **Adikavi**, the "first poet" of Kannada. He wrote the *Adipurana* and the *Vikramarjuna Vijaya*, also known as the *Pampa Bharata*, his telling of the Mahabharata with Arjuna as the hero.

**Remember:** Pampa is the poet the song starts with, the beginning of the journey.

## 2. Basavanna (ಬಸವಣ್ಣ), poems anyone could understand

In the **12th century**, Basavanna, a minister at Kalyana, chose to write short sayings called **vachanas** in everyday Kannada instead of in a scholars' language. His vachanas speak of honest work, kindness and treating every person as equal. He also gathered thinkers in a hall of discussion known as the **Anubhava Mantapa**.

**Remember:** a vachana is short, clear and meant for everyone.

## 3. Akka Mahadevi (ಅಕ್ಕಮಹಾದೇವಿ), the brave voice

Akka Mahadevi came from **Udutadi**, in today's Shivamogga district, and lived in the same 12th-century age as Basavanna. Her vachanas are full of nature (birds, bees, forests and hills) and of her love for **Chennamallikarjuna**, her name for Shiva. She left a comfortable life to follow what she believed in, and she is one of the earliest women poets in Kannada.

**Remember:** "Akka" means elder sister, and that is how people still speak of her.

## 4. Sarvajna (ಸರ್ವಜ್ಞ), wisdom in three lines

Sarvajna is believed to have lived around the **16th century**. He wrote **tripadis**, poems of just three lines, about honesty, food, friendship, hard work and common sense. Many people in Karnataka quote a Sarvajna line without knowing it is his.

**Remember:** three lines, one lesson. Tripadi literally means "three feet" (three lines).

## 5. Bendre (ದ. ರಾ. ಬೇಂದ್ರೆ), the poet of Dharwad

**D. R. Bendre** (1896–1981) wrote poems that sound like songs, full of rhythm and village life, and many of them are still sung today. He received the **Jnanpith Award** for his collection *Naaku Tanti* ("Four Strings").

**Remember:** if a Kannada poem makes you want to sing, it might be Bendre.

## 6. Kuvempu (ಕುವೆಂಪು), the poet of the universal human

**Kuvempu** is the pen name of **K. V. Puttappa** (1904–1994), who grew up in Kuppalli in the Malnad hills. He wrote Karnataka's state song *Jaya Bharata Jananiya Tanujate*, and in 1967 he became the **first Kannada writer to receive the Jnanpith Award**, for his epic *Sri Ramayana Darshanam*. His big idea was **Vishwa Manava**, the "universal human": every child is born a citizen of the whole world, and we should not shrink ourselves into small groups.

**Remember:** Kuvempu is where the song's journey arrives, and his message is "be a universal human."

## Why this matters for children

A thousand years of poems is not a museum. Each of these poets did something brave with words: Pampa told an old story in a new way, Basavanna and Akka Mahadevi wrote for ordinary people, Sarvajna packed wisdom into three lines, Bendre made poems you can sing, and Kuvempu asked us to think beyond borders. Children who meet these names early feel that their language belongs to them, wherever they live.

## One small thing to try today

- **Play the song together** and count how many poets' names you can hear.
- **Write a family tripadi:** three short lines, in Kannada or any language, about something you learnt today. Read it at dinner.
- **Ask a grandparent** which Kannada poem or song they learnt in school. Write down the first line.

## FAQ

### Who is called the Adikavi of Kannada?

Pampa, the 10th-century poet who wrote the *Adipurana* and the *Vikramarjuna Vijaya* (*Pampa Bharata*).

### Who was the first Kannada writer to win the Jnanpith Award?

Kuvempu (K. V. Puttappa), in 1967, for *Sri Ramayana Darshanam*.

### What is a vachana?

A short saying or prose-poem in simple Kannada, written by Basavanna, Akka Mahadevi and other sharanas of the 12th century.

### When is Kannada Rajyotsava?

On **1 November** every year. It marks the day in 1956 when the Kannada-speaking regions were brought together into one state, which was renamed Karnataka in 1973.
