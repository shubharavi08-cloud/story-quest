In mid-November, as the Malayalam month of **Vrischikam** begins, millions of families in Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana start getting ready for the **Mandala season** of **Sabarimala**. Fathers, uncles and grandfathers put on a bead **mala**, wear black or blue, and suddenly everyone calls them "**Swami**". Children notice, and they ask: *who is Ayyappa, and why do people do this?*

Here is the story as it is traditionally told at home, in a form a child can follow, with our Kannada song ***ಹುಲಿಯೇರಿ ಬಂದ ಮಣಿಕಂಠ · Huli Yeri Banda Manikanta*** above.

## 1. A baby by the river Pampa

The story says that **King Rajasekhara of Pandalam**, a small kingdom in Kerala, had no children. One day, while hunting in the forest near the river **Pampa**, he heard a baby crying. He found a glowing child with a little **bell (mani) tied around his neck (kanta)**. The king took him home and named him **Manikanta**, "the one with the bell at his throat".

**Remember:** Manikanta = mani (bell) + kanta (neck).

## 2. Son of Hari and Hara

Tradition holds that Manikanta was born of **Shiva (Hara)** and **Vishnu (Hari)** in his form as **Mohini**. That is why one of his names is **Hariharasuta** (हरिहरसुत, ಹರಿಹರಸುತ), "son of Hari and Hara". Our song *Harihara Suthane* is named after this.

**Remember:** Ayyappa brings two great traditions together in one child.

## 3. The ride on the tigress

Manikanta grew up as a clever, kind prince. The queen later had a son of her own, and a jealous minister persuaded her to pretend to be very ill. The palace physician said only **the milk of a tigress** could cure her. Young Manikanta set off into the forest alone, and the story says he came back **riding a tigress**, with her cubs following, to the amazement of the whole kingdom. This is the scene of *Huli Yeri Banda Manikanta*, "Manikanta who came riding a tiger".

**Remember:** courage, and kindness to the very people who tried to trick him. He forgave them.

## 4. Mahishi and the journey to the hills

Manikanta's real task, the tradition says, was to end the demoness **Mahishi**, who troubled the gods and people. After defeating her, he told the king he must leave the palace. He asked for a temple on the hill of **Sabarimala**, in the forests of the Western Ghats, and there he is worshipped as **Ayyappa**, seated in meditation.

Along the way Ayyappa made a friend, **Vavar**. Pilgrims honour Vavar too, at a shrine at **Erumeli** and near the main temple, so the pilgrimage itself remembers friendship across communities.

## 5. The 41-day vratham

Before going to Sabarimala, devotees keep a **vratham** (vow) for about **41 days**, the Mandala period. They wear a mala, dress simply in black or blue, eat simple vegetarian food, wake early, pray, avoid anger and harsh words, and call one another "Swami", because the tradition says the divine lives in every person. They carry an **irumudi**, a two-part cloth bundle with offerings, on their heads, chanting **ಸ್ವಾಮಿಯೇ ಶರಣಂ ಅಯ್ಯಪ್ಪ · Swamiye Sharanam Ayyappa**.

**Remember:** the vratham is about self-control and treating everyone as equal, not only about the journey.

## 6. The 18 sacred steps

At the temple, pilgrims who have kept the vow climb the **Pathinettam Padi**, the **18 sacred steps**. Families explain the number in many ways: some link the steps to the 18 hills around Sabarimala, others to qualities a person must rise above. Our song *Pathinettam Padi* is about this climb. The season ends with **Makaravilakku** on **Makara Sankranti** in mid-January.

## Why this story matters for children

Ayyappa's story is full of things children understand: a lost baby who is loved, a brave errand into the forest, forgiveness for those who were unkind, a friend from a different background, and a vow where everyone is called by the same respectful name. Whether or not your family makes the pilgrimage, these are values worth talking about at the dinner table.

## One small thing to try today

- **Play the song together** and ask your child to spot the tiger, the forest and the hill in the words.
- **Count 18 steps** on your staircase or in the park, and at each step name one good habit (sharing, saying sorry, helping at home).
- **Call each other "Swami" for one meal**, and talk about why the vratham asks everyone to do this.

## FAQ

### Why is Ayyappa called Manikanta?

Because, in the story, King Rajasekhara found him as a baby with a bell (*mani*) around his neck (*kanta*).

### What is the Mandala season?

The pilgrimage season of Sabarimala, which begins in the Malayalam month of Vrischikam (mid-November) and lasts about 41 days, ending with the Mandala Pooja in late December. The temple opens again for Makaravilakku in mid-January.

### What are the 18 steps of Sabarimala?

The *Pathinettam Padi*, the sacred flight of 18 steps leading to the shrine, climbed by pilgrims who have kept the vratham.
