Every child knows the feeling. A test tomorrow, a dog barking at the gate, a new class where nobody knows your name. The first instinct is to run. A story from Swami Vivekananda's own life shows why running so often makes the fear bigger, and the Bhagavad Gita gives the same advice in a single line.

## The monkeys of Banaras

When Swami Vivekananda was a young wandering monk, he was once walking in Banaras (Varanasi) along a road with a large water tank on one side and a high wall on the other. A troop of monkeys lived there. They did not want him to pass, and they began to chase him, screaming and grabbing at his feet. He ran. The faster he ran, the closer they came.

Then an old sannyasi called out to him: **"Face the brutes."** Vivekananda stopped, turned round and faced them. The monkeys fell back and ran away.

Years later, Vivekananda told this story in his talks to make one point: when we run from a difficulty, it chases us; when we turn and face it, it often loses its power. Face the brutes, face difficulty, and do not run from it.

## What Gita 2.3 says

At the start of the Bhagavad Gita, Arjuna stands on the battlefield of Kurukshetra, sees his own teachers and relatives on the other side, drops his bow and sits down, overcome. Krishna's first real words of advice are a wake-up call:

<p lang="sa" style="font-size:1.25em;text-align:center;line-height:1.7;margin:18px 0 6px">क्लैब्यं मा स्म गमः पार्थ नैतत्त्वय्युपपद्यते।<br>क्षुद्रं हृदयदौर्बल्यं त्यक्त्वोत्तिष्ठ परन्तप॥</p>
<p style="text-align:center;color:var(--muted);font-size:.95em;margin:0 0 16px">klaibyaṁ mā sma gamaḥ pārtha naitat tvayy upapadyate · kṣudraṁ hṛdaya-daurbalyaṁ tyaktvottiṣṭha parantapa<br>Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2, Verse 3</p>

> **Meaning:** "Do not give in to this weakness, Partha (Arjuna); it does not suit you. Cast off this petty weakness of the heart, and stand up, O scorcher of foes."

Krishna does not say the fear is not real. He says it is not who Arjuna is. The word *uttiṣṭha*, "stand up", is the same call Vivekananda later made famous with "Arise, awake".

## Why it matters for children today

The monkeys of today look different: a maths chapter that feels too hard, a stage performance, an unkind message online, a phone that keeps pulling us away from what we need to do. Running away (avoiding, hiding, scrolling) feels safe for a moment, but the fear grows the next time.

Facing it does not mean being fearless. It means:

- **Naming the fear out loud:** "I am scared of the science test."
- **Taking one small step towards it:** opening the chapter, doing just one question.
- **Asking for help:** a parent, a teacher or a friend standing beside you is like that old sannyasi calling out.

## FAQ

### Is the Vivekananda monkey story true?

Yes. Swami Vivekananda narrated it himself in his lectures, and it is recorded in *The Complete Works of Swami Vivekananda* (Advaita Ashrama).

### Which chapter and verse of the Gita is "kṣudraṁ hṛdaya-daurbalyaṁ"?

Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2, Verse 3, spoken by Krishna to Arjuna at the beginning of the teaching.
