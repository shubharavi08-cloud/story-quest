Who Is Kadutha Swami? The Warrior Who Guards Sabarimala's 18 Holy Steps, Explained for Children

Songs26 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Watch & take your step

Every pilgrim who reaches the top of Sabarimala comes face to face with the most famous staircase in Kerala: the Pathinettam Padi, the 18 holy steps that lead up to Ayyappa's shrine. Pilgrims break a coconut, call out Swamiye Saranam Ayyappa and climb, the Irumudi on their heads. But before they climb, many of them stop and bow to two guardians who stand on either side of the steps. One of them is a warrior named Kadutha (ಕಡುತ್ತ), whom devotees call Kadutha Swami.

1. A warrior of the hills

In the stories Ayyappa devotees tell, Kadutha was a great warrior of the hill country around Pandalam. He was strong, fearless and good with weapons, and he was devoted to young Manikanta, the prince of Pandalam who would become Ayyappa.

In those days the forest roads were not safe. Robber bands troubled travellers and villages, and Manikanta set out to protect the people. Kadutha fought beside him, a trusted helper in battle, and in many tellings one of the bravest of all Manikanta's companions.

2. Why he stays at the steps

When Manikanta's work on earth was done and he chose to sit in deep meditation at Sabarimala, his loyal companions did not go home. The tradition says Kadutha stayed on as a guardian of the holy steps, watching over everyone who comes to see Ayyappa.

That is why, at the foot of the Pathinettam Padi, there is a shrine to Kadutha Swami on one side and a shrine to Karuppa Swami, another fierce guardian, on the other. Pilgrims pray to them before they climb, just as you might greet the elders at the door of a house before you go in.

3. What are the 18 steps?

The 18 steps are the holiest part of the temple. Only pilgrims who have kept the 41-day vratham and carry the Irumudi climb them; others use a different path. Families and Guruswamis explain the number 18 in many ways. A popular explanation says the steps stand for the things a pilgrim learns to rise above on the way to God: the five senses, eight feelings such as anger, greed and pride, the three qualities (sattva, rajas and tamas), and finally knowledge and ignorance. Climbing the steps is like climbing above all of them, one step at a time.

4. Friends around Ayyappa

Kadutha is not the only companion honoured at Sabarimala. Vavar, who began as Manikanta's rival and became his best friend, has his own shrine, Vavar Nada, close to the steps (read Who Is Vavar Swami?). Malikappurathamma has her temple nearby (read Who Is Malikappurathamma?). Together they show children something lovely about Sabarimala: Ayyappa is surrounded by the people who stood by him.

A lesson for children: loyalty

Kadutha did not stay because someone ordered him to. He stayed because he cared about his friend and about the people who would come to see him. That is loyalty: standing by someone, and by what is right, even when nobody is watching.

Talk about it with your child:

  • "Who stands by me when things are hard? Do I stand by them?"
  • "What is one job at home or at school that I can look after faithfully, every day?"
  • "How can I help someone who is new, the way Kadutha helps the pilgrims?"

FAQ

Who is Kadutha Swami?

In the Ayyappa tradition, Kadutha was a brave warrior who fought beside Manikanta and became a guardian of the 18 holy steps at Sabarimala. His shrine stands at the foot of the Pathinettam Padi.

Who is on the other side of the 18 steps?

Karuppa Swami, another guardian deity. Pilgrims honour both guardians before they climb.

Can everyone climb the 18 steps?

The 18 steps are climbed by pilgrims who have kept the 41-day vratham and carry the Irumudi. Other visitors reach the shrine by a separate path. Ask your Guruswami or check the Travancore Devaswom Board for the current rules.
