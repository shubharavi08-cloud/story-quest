Swamiye Saranam Ayyappa Meaning for Children: Why Every Sabarimala Pilgrim Is Called 'Swami'

Songs18 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Watch & take your step

If you live in South India, you have probably heard it this month: a group of pilgrims in black or blue clothes, one voice calling "Swamiye!" and everyone answering "Saranam Ayyappa!" Children pick it up very quickly, and then they ask the best question of all: what does it actually mean?

Here is the chant explained word by word, the way it is traditionally told at home, with our Kannada song ಹದಿನೆಂಟಾಂ ಪಡಿ · Pathinettam Padi above. It is the song of the 18 sacred steps, where this chant rings the loudest.

1. The chant, word by word

| Word | Meaning | |---|---| | Swamiye | "O Swami", O Lord (the -ye is the calling form, like saying "O…!") | | Saranam | refuge, shelter, the place where you are safe (Sanskrit शरणम्) | | Ayyappa | the name of the Lord of Sabarimala |

So Swamiye Saranam Ayyappa means: "O Lord Ayyappa, you are my refuge." In other words: "I am safe with you."

Families say it in their own language and script:

  • Kannada: ಸ್ವಾಮಿಯೇ ಶರಣಂ ಅಯ್ಯಪ್ಪ
  • Tamil: சுவாமியே சரணம் ஐயப்பா
  • Telugu: స్వామియే శరణం అయ్యప్ప
  • Malayalam: സ്വാമിയേ ശരണം അയ്യപ്പാ
  • Devanagari: स्वामिये शरणम् अय्यप्पा

Remember: saranam = refuge. The whole chant is one short promise: "I trust you."

2. Why is it a call and an answer?

On the long walk to Sabarimala, one pilgrim calls out and the whole group answers. This calling out is known as saranam vili (the "calling of saranam"). It does three things at once:

  • It keeps the group together. In a forest or a crowd, you know your group by its voice.
  • It gives tired legs a rhythm. Call, answer, step. Call, answer, step.
  • It turns a hard climb into a prayer. Instead of saying "I'm tired", the pilgrim says "Saranam".

Pilgrims also make up their own lines in the same pattern as they walk, such as "Pallikattu, Sabarimalaikku" (with the irumudi on our heads, to Sabarimala), and everyone answers "Kallum mullum, kalukku methai" (stones and thorns are a soft mattress for our feet). Children love this one, because it sounds like a brave little game.

Remember: one voice calls, many voices answer. Nobody climbs alone.

3. Why do pilgrims call each other "Swami"?

During the vratham, the weeks of simple living before the pilgrimage, something lovely happens. A pilgrim does not call his friend by name, or "uncle", or "sir". He calls him Swami. A first-time pilgrim is lovingly called Kanni Swami.

Why? Because of two words that greet pilgrims at Sabarimala: Tattvamasi (तत्त्वमसि). They come from the Chandogya Upanishad (6.8.7), where a father, Uddalaka, teaches his son Shvetaketu, and they mean "You are That." The way it is told to children: the goodness you are climbing the hill to see is also inside you, and inside everyone walking next to you.

So if Ayyappa is Swami, and Ayyappa's light is in every pilgrim, then every pilgrim is Swami too. Rich or poor, young or old, everyone wears the same simple clothes, eats the same simple food, and is called by the same name.

Remember: Tattvamasi, "you are That". Treat everyone as if God were standing in front of you.

4. Saranam is an old, big idea

The word śaraṇam is much older than the chant. In the Bhagavad Gita (18.66), Krishna tells Arjuna "मामेकं शरणं व्रज" (mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja), "take refuge in Me alone". The idea is the same in both: when a task is too big for us, we do our part with full effort and leave the worry with God.

For a child, that might mean: study well for a test, then go to sleep without fear. Practise the dance, then step on the stage with a smile. Do your part, and say saranam for the rest.

One small thing to try today

  • Play call and answer at home. One person calls "Swamiye!", everyone answers "Saranam Ayyappa!" Then let the youngest child lead.
  • Make a family "saranam" line in the same pattern about something hard today: "Homework is long!" "Saranam, we'll finish it!"
  • Play our free game Saranam Echo and keep the temple rhythm, then climb the steps in Sharanam Steps.
  • Count the names of Ayyappa bead by bead in 108 Names Mala.

FAQ

What is the meaning of Swamiye Saranam Ayyappa?

"O Lord Ayyappa, you are my refuge." Swamiye means "O Lord", saranam means refuge or shelter, and Ayyappa is the Lord of Sabarimala.

Why do Ayyappa devotees call each other Swami?

Because of the teaching Tattvamasi, "You are That": the divine is seen in every pilgrim, so during the vratham everyone addresses everyone else as Swami.

Can children say Swamiye Saranam Ayyappa?

Yes. It is a simple prayer of trust, and families sing it with children of every age at home, in bhajans and on the pilgrimage.
