Who Is Vavar Swami? How Ayyappa's Enemy Became His Best Friend, Explained for Children

Songs23 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

Watch & take your step

Before most pilgrims even begin the walk to Sabarimala, they stop at a small, busy place in the town of Erumeli. It is not a temple. It is a mosque, and pilgrims in black dhotis, with the Irumudi on their heads, walk around it, bow and pray before going on. At the top of the hill, right beside the 18 holy steps, there is another shrine to the same friend. His name is Vavar (ವಾವರ), and devotees lovingly call him Vavar Swami.

How did a friend of Ayyappa come to have a mosque at the start of the pilgrimage and a shrine at the very end? It all begins with a fight.

1. The warrior from across the sea

In the tellings of Ayyappa devotees, Vavar came to the coast of Kerala from across the sea. Many families remember him as a brave Muslim warrior and sailor; some call him a chieftain who led his own band of fighters. He was strong, fearless and proud, and the forests and roads near Erumeli were his ground.

At that time young Manikanta, the prince of Pandalam, was protecting the people of the hills from danger. Sooner or later, the two strongest young men of the land were bound to meet.

Our song Kadalacheya Dheera (the brave one from across the sea) at 3:53 in the satsang tells how Vavar arrived.

2. From a fight to a friendship

When they met, Vavar raised his sword against Manikanta. The fight was long and hard. But as it went on, something surprising happened. Vavar saw that this boy did not fight with hate. He fought calmly, without cruelty, and only to protect others. Manikanta, in turn, saw that Vavar was brave and honest, a man who could be a great friend if only the anger were set aside.

By the end of the fight, the tradition says, there was no winner and no loser. Vavar put down his sword and became Manikanta's closest friend and helper. From then on they stood side by side, and Vavar helped Manikanta keep the forest paths safe for travellers.

Our song Geleya Vavara (friend Vavar) at 7:16 sings this moment. Our short video, Who is Vavar?, tells it in one minute in Kannada.

3. Friends together

The story does not stop at the handshake. Vavar stayed with Manikanta through his adventures in the forest, including the journey that led to the defeat of Mahishi near Erumeli. (Read that part in Petta Thullal at Erumeli, and what happened next in Who Is Malikappurathamma?.)

When Manikanta finally went to Sabarimala to sit in deep meditation, his friend was not forgotten. The tradition says Ayyappa wished that Vavar should always be close by, and that the pilgrims who came to see him should honour his friend too.

Our song Onde Belaku (one light) at 10:53 says it simply: different names, one light.

4. The Vavar mosque at Erumeli

That wish is still kept today. At Erumeli, where pilgrims dance the colourful Petta Thullal, stands the Vavar mosque (in Malayalam, Vavar Palli). Pilgrims walk to it, go around it and pray, and then carry on to the Dharma Sastha temple in the same town. For many groups it is the very first stop of the pilgrimage.

Think about how special that is. Every year, lakhs of Ayyappa devotees begin their journey by visiting a mosque, and the people who look after the mosque welcome them. Few places in the world show friendship across faiths so openly, year after year.

5. Vavar Nada at Sabarimala

At the top of the hill, at the Sannidhanam, there is a shrine called Vavar Nada, right beside the 18 holy steps (the Pathinettam Padi). So Vavar is there at the start of the journey and at the end. The friend who once raised his sword now stands by the steps, as if welcoming every pilgrim who climbs up to see Ayyappa.

Our song Mettila Pakkada Geleya (the friend beside the steps) at 18:56 tells this last part.

A lesson for children: an enemy can become a friend

Manikanta could have defeated Vavar and walked away. Instead, he fought without hate and left room for friendship. Vavar could have stayed proud. Instead, he was brave enough to say, "I was wrong to fight you."

Talk about it with your child:

  • "Is there someone I argued with who could become my friend?"
  • "When I am angry, can I stop and see the good in the other person?"
  • "Do I have friends who pray differently from my family? What do we share?"

The children's song in the satsang, Modalu Vavara (Vavar first) at 16:40, is an easy one to learn and sing together.

FAQ

Who was Vavar Swami?

In the Ayyappa tradition, Vavar was a warrior from across the sea who first fought Manikanta and then became his closest friend and helper. Many devotees remember him as a Muslim, and he is honoured at the Vavar mosque in Erumeli and at Vavar Nada at Sabarimala.

Why do Ayyappa pilgrims visit a mosque?

Because the story says Ayyappa wanted his friend Vavar to be honoured by everyone who came to him. Visiting the Vavar mosque at Erumeli is a long custom of the pilgrimage, and many groups make it their first stop.

Where is Vavar Nada?

At the Sannidhanam, the hilltop temple complex of Sabarimala, close to the 18 holy steps.
