Why Did Manikanta Bring Tiger's Milk? The Ayyappa Tiger Story, Explained for Children

Songs24 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Watch & take your step

Look at almost any picture of Swami Ayyappa riding out of a forest and you will see the same thing: a young boy, calm and smiling, sitting on the back of a huge tiger, with more tigers walking behind him. Children always ask the same question: why is he on a tiger?

The answer is one of the best-loved stories of the Ayyappa tradition, the story of ಹುಲಿಯ ಹಾಲು, the tiger's milk. Our Kannada satsang above, ಹುಲಿಯ ಹಾಲು ತಂದ 12 ವರ್ಷದ ಮಣಿಕಂಠ, tells it in six parts with Guruji and the children, with Kannada, Tamil, Telugu and Malayalam Ayyappa songs in between. Watch it together, then read on.

1. A boy who gave the best gift to his teacher

Baby Manikanta was found by King Rajasekhara of Pandalam on the banks of the Pampa river, and the king and queen raised him as their own son. When he was old enough, he was sent to a gurukula to study.

At the end of his studies came the time for guru dakshina (गुरुदक्षिणा, ಗುರುದಕ್ಷಿಣೆ), the gift a student gives to the teacher. Manikanta's teacher had a son who could not see or speak, and the teacher's only wish was for his son to be well. In the tellings of devotees, Manikanta blessed the boy and he was healed. The young prince's first gift to the world was kindness. (This part begins at 1:15 in the satsang.)

2. The queen's fear

Back at the palace, the queen now had a baby son of her own. A jealous minister began to whisper to her: "When the king grows old, Manikanta will become king, not your own son." Slowly, fear filled the queen's heart.

The minister made a plan. The queen would pretend to have a terrible headache, and a physician who was part of the plan would say that only one medicine could cure it: the milk of a tigress. No one could go into the deep forest and come back alive with tiger's milk, or so they thought. (Part 2, from 9:18.)

3. A 12-year-old walks into the forest

When Manikanta heard that his mother was in pain, he did not ask whether the story was true. He did not complain that the task was impossible. He simply said he would go.

The king begged him not to; Manikanta was only about twelve. But the boy insisted, and walked into the forest, many tellings say barefoot, with a few simple things packed for the road. Pilgrims remember this when they carry their own bundle, the Irumudi, on the way to Sabarimala. (Parts 3 and 4, from 15:11 and 25:04.)

4. The tigers bow

Deep in the forest, something wonderful happened. The wild animals did not attack the boy. The tigers bowed to him. In the tellings of devotees, Indra, king of the devas, came in the form of a great tiger, and the other devas came as tigers and tigresses, so that Manikanta would have all the milk he needed. (Part 5, from 36:51; our song Huli Yeri Banda Manikanta follows at 38:56.)

In many family tellings, this same journey is when Manikanta also defeats the demoness Mahishi. In our satsang that battle is the next day's story: Day 4, Mahishi and Manikanta at Erumeli.

5. Riding home to Pandalam

Then came the sight the people of Pandalam never forgot: their young prince riding out of the forest on the back of a tiger, with a whole line of tigers walking behind him.

The minister was terrified. The queen understood that her fear had been wrong and that this was no ordinary child. The king, overjoyed, saw at last that his son was divine. And Manikanta? He did not shout or punish anyone. He forgave them. (Part 6, from 47:39.)

This is the moment shown in the famous pictures, and it is why one of Ayyappa's names in songs is the one who rides the tiger.

6. What the story teaches children

  • Courage is doing the right thing even when it is hard. Manikanta did not wait for someone braver to go.
  • Care for your parents. He went because his mother said she was in pain.
  • Fear makes people do unkind things. The queen was not wicked at heart; she was afraid. Talking about our fears helps.
  • Forgiveness is strength. The boy who could command tigers chose to forgive.

FAQ

Why is Ayyappa shown riding a tiger?

Because of the tiger's milk story: young Manikanta went into the forest to bring tigress's milk for his mother's "headache" and returned to Pandalam riding a tiger, with the devas following him as tigers.

How old was Manikanta when he brought the tiger's milk?

The tradition, and our satsang, say he was about twelve years old.

Was the queen's headache real?

In the traditional telling, no. A jealous minister persuaded the queen to pretend, hoping Manikanta would never come back from the forest. When he returned on a tiger, they realised their mistake and he forgave them.
