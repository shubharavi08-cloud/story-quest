What Is Mala Dharana? The 41-Day Ayyappa Mandala Vratham Explained for Children

Songs20 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Watch & take your step

In the middle of November, children in many South Indian homes notice a small change. Appa, a grandfather or an uncle wakes up earlier, bathes before sunrise, wears black or blue clothes, and has a new string of beads around his neck. Ask him why, and he will smile and say: "Maale haakiddini" (ಮಾಲೆ ಹಾಕಿದ್ದೀನಿ, "I have worn the mala"). The 41 days that follow are called the Mandala vratham, and the first step of it is Mala Dharana, the wearing of the mala.

1. What does "Mala Dharana" mean?

The words are simple:

| Word | Meaning | |---|---| | Mala | a string of beads | | Dharana | wearing, holding, carrying | | Mandala | a circle, here a full cycle of 41 days | | Vratham | a promise kept with discipline |

So Mala Dharana means wearing the mala, and the Mandala vratham is a 41-day promise. The mala is usually made of tulasi or rudraksha beads with a small locket of Swami Ayyappa. It is worn after a short prayer, often at a temple, and is traditionally placed on the devotee's neck by a Guruswami, an elder who has made the Sabarimala pilgrimage many times and guides the new pilgrims.

From that moment the devotee is called Swami, and he calls everyone else Swami too. (You can read why in our post on Swamiye Saranam Ayyappa.)

2. Why 41 days?

The Mandala season at Sabarimala is about 41 days long and ends with the Mandala Pooja. In 2026 it runs from about 17 November to 27 December. A devotee who plans to climb to the shrine keeps the vratham for these 41 days, or at least for 41 days before the journey.

Here is a way to explain it to a child: a good habit becomes part of you if you keep it every single day for a long time. Forty-one days is long enough for the mind to become quiet and steady. The mala around the neck is a reminder, every time you feel it, of the promise you made.

3. What does the vratham ask?

Families follow it in their own ways, but the heart of it is the same in most homes:

  • A bath before prayer, morning and evening, and lighting a lamp at home.
  • Simple, home-cooked vegetarian food, eaten with gratitude.
  • Plain black, blue or saffron clothes, so that everyone looks alike. Rich or poor, all are Swamis.
  • Kind words only. No anger, no fighting, no lying, no harsh talk.
  • Saying "Swamiye Saranam Ayyappa" many times a day, and joining the evening bhajans.
  • Living simply: many devotees walk barefoot and sleep on a mat on the floor.

Notice that most of these are not about the body at all. They are about the mind: speaking gently, keeping calm, treating every person as Swami. That is the real mala.

4. Who is a Kanni Swami?

A devotee going to Sabarimala for the first time is called a Kanni Swami (kanni means new or first). Kanni Swamis get extra care from the Guruswami and the group. In many groups, children who go with their families, called Kutti Swamis or Manikantas, are the youngest members. Everyone looks after them on the long walk.

5. How the mala comes off

At the end of the pilgrimage, after the darshan of Swami Ayyappa, the devotee returns home and removes the mala with a prayer, often in front of the Guruswami or at a temple. The promise has been kept. Many people say the best part is not the end, but what stays: the habit of getting up early, of praying, of speaking kindly.

A lesson for children: your own small mala

Children do not need to keep the full vratham to learn from it. Pick one small promise for the Mandala season, just like a mala:

  • "I will say thank you before every meal."
  • "I will not shout at my little brother."
  • "I will help Amma lay the table every evening."

Write it on a card, hang it near the prayer lamp, and keep it for 41 days. That is your mala.

FAQ

Why do Ayyappa devotees wear black?

Black, blue or saffron clothes show that the wearer is keeping the vratham, and they make everyone look the same, so that no one is "more important" on the path to Sabarimala.

Can children wear the Ayyappa mala?

Yes, in many families children who travel with their parents wear the mala as Kutti Swamis, guided by the Guruswami. Each family and group follows its own custom.

When does the Mandala season end?

It ends with the Mandala Pooja at Sabarimala, about 41 days after it begins. The Makaravilakku festival follows in mid-January.
