Steady Hands in the Storm: The Potter, Calm Action and Bhagavad Gita 2.48

A Thought a Day12 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

Some days everything around us is noisy: marks being compared, a phone buzzing, someone praising us one minute and criticising us the next. Children feel this as strongly as adults do. A simple picture of a potter at his wheel, and one line from the Bhagavad Gita, show a way to stay calm without stopping work.

The potter and the wind

A potter sits quietly at his wheel. Outside, the wind is gusting, dust is flying and the street is full of noise. But his hands stay steady on the clay.

If his attention jumped with every gust, the pot would lose its shape. He cannot stop the wind. What he can do is keep his hands steady on the work in front of him, and that is exactly what makes a good pot.

This is a parable, a small made-up story, but every child who has tried to finish homework in a noisy house knows that it is true.

What Gita 2.48 says

On the battlefield of Kurukshetra, Arjuna is worried about what will happen: victory, defeat, praise, blame. Krishna tells him to put his whole heart into the work and let go of that worry:

योगस्थः कुरु कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा धनञ्जय।
सिद्ध्यसिद्ध्योः समो भूत्वा समत्वं योग उच्यते॥

yoga-sthaḥ kuru karmāṇi saṅgaṁ tyaktvā dhanañjaya · siddhy-asiddhyoḥ samo bhūtvā samatvaṁ yoga ucyate
Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2, Verse 48

Meaning: "Steady in yoga, do your work, O Dhananjaya (Arjuna), giving up attachment. Stay the same in success and failure. This evenness of mind is called yoga."

The key word is samatvam, evenness. Krishna is not telling Arjuna to stop caring about his work. He is telling him to care about the work itself, and not to be thrown about by the result.

Why it matters for children today

The "wind" for a child today might be:

  • Comparison: "She scored more than me."
  • Praise and criticism: feeling on top of the world one minute and crushed the next.
  • Rush: too many classes, too little time.
  • Things we cannot predict: a hard question paper, a match that goes the wrong way.

Samatvam does not mean not feeling anything. It means coming back to the clay: the next question, the next page, the next practice ball.

One small step for today

Pick one task today, a chapter, a drawing, a chore, and do it with your whole attention for 15 minutes. Phone away, no checking how others are doing. When the time is up, notice how it felt. Families can do this together after dinner and share one sentence each: "My gust of wind today was…"

FAQ

Is the potter story from the Gita?

No. The potter is a simple parable we use to explain the verse. The teaching itself is Bhagavad Gita 2.48, spoken by Krishna to Arjuna.

What does "yogasthah kuru karmani" mean?

"Established in yoga, do your work." The verse goes on to define yoga as samatvam, evenness of mind in success and failure.

Is this helpful for exam stress?

Yes. Many students lose marks not from lack of study but from panic. Focusing on the question in front of you, instead of the final result, is the practical form of this verse.
