Lift Yourself: Meera's Bicycle and Bhagavad Gita 6.5 for Children

A Thought a Day13 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

Every child knows the moment: you try something new, it goes wrong, and a small voice inside says "I can't do this." Sometimes that voice is louder than any teacher or parent. A little girl on a bicycle, and one verse from the Bhagavad Gita, show us that we can answer that voice.

Meera and the bicycle

Meera is learning to ride a bicycle in her lane. Her grandfather walks beside her, holding the back of the seat. She wobbles, the handle turns, and she falls. Her knee is scraped.

She sits in the dust and says, "I can't do this. I'm just not good at it."

Her grandfather smiles. "I can hold the seat. But only you can push the pedals."

That is the whole story, and it is a true one for every learner. A grandparent, a teacher or a coach can hold the seat for a while. Nobody else can pedal for us. This is a parable, a small made-up story, but anyone who has learnt to ride, swim, write or speak in front of a class knows how real it is.

What Gita 6.5 says

In the sixth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna tells Arjuna that the mind can lift us up or pull us down:

उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत्।
आत्मैव ह्यात्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव रिपुरात्मनः॥

uddhared ātmanātmānaṁ nātmānam avasādayet · ātmaiva hy ātmano bandhur ātmaiva ripur ātmanaḥ
Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 6, Verse 5

Meaning: "Lift yourself up by your own mind; do not let yourself sink down. For the mind alone is your friend, and the mind alone is your enemy."

Two words carry the verse:

  • uddharet, let one lift up: the work of rising is ours.
  • bandhu and ripu, friend and enemy: the same mind can play either part, depending on how we talk to ourselves.

Krishna is not saying that nobody else can help. Meera's grandfather really does help. He is saying that the last push, the decision to get back on, has to come from inside.

Why it matters for children today

The voice that pulls children down often sounds like this:

  • "I'm just not good at maths." One bad test becomes a label.
  • "Everyone else can do it." Comparison makes one fall feel like a verdict.
  • "What if I fail again?" Fear of the next fall stops the next try.

The verse gives a simple answer: become your own best friend. A best friend does not lie and say the fall did not happen. A best friend says, "That hurt. Let's try once more."

One small step for today

Today, when a voice inside says "I can't", answer it out loud: "I'll try once more."

Families can make this a game at dinner. Each person shares one thing they found hard today, and what their "best friend voice" would say about it. Parents go first: children learn self-talk mostly by hearing how adults talk to themselves.

FAQ

Is the Meera story from the Gita?

No. Meera and her grandfather are a simple parable we use to explain the verse. The teaching is Bhagavad Gita 6.5, spoken by Krishna to Arjuna.

What does "uddhared atmanatmanam" mean?

"One should lift oneself up by oneself." The verse continues: do not let yourself sink, because the mind can be your friend or your enemy.

How can I explain this verse to a young child?

Use the bicycle. Ask: "Who can hold the seat?" (anyone who loves you) and "Who has to push the pedals?" (only you). Then say the first line of the shloka together.
