What Is the Irumudi? Ayyappa's Sacred Two-Part Bundle Explained for Children

Songs17 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Watch & take your step

As the Mandala season begins, children in many South Indian homes see something new: an uncle or grandfather sitting with a cloth bundle that has two pockets, a coconut being filled with ghee, and everyone chanting Swamiye Saranam Ayyappa. That bundle is the irumudi (ಇರುಮುಡಿ · இருமுடி), and a Sabarimala pilgrim carries it on the head all the way to the hill shrine.

Here is what it is, what goes inside, and the small lesson it holds, explained the way it is traditionally told at home, with our Kannada song ಕರುಣಾಮಯ ಅಯ್ಯಪ್ಪ · Karunamaya Ayyappa above.

1. What does "irumudi" mean?

The word comes from iru (two) and mudi (a tied bundle). So irumudi simply means "the bundle with two parts". It is usually a long cloth bag in black, blue or saffron, tied in the middle so that it makes two pockets, one in front and one behind.

Remember: iru = two, mudi = bundle. Two parts, one journey.

2. The front part: for Ayyappa

The front pocket, called the munmudi, holds the offerings for the temple. The most important one is a coconut filled with pure ghee. Families also pack things used in worship, such as rice, turmeric, kumkum, sandal paste, camphor, incense, betel leaves and areca nut, and a small offering of coins. Each family's list is a little different.

Remember: the front part is what the pilgrim gives.

3. The back part: for the pilgrim

The back pocket, the pinmudi, holds what the pilgrim needs for the journey: simple food like rice and a few things for the road. In the old days the walk through the forest took days, so this part really mattered.

Remember: the back part is what the pilgrim needs.

4. Kettu Nira: filling the bundle

The irumudi is not packed like a school bag. It is filled in a small ceremony called Kettu Nira (also called Irumudi Kettu), usually led by an experienced pilgrim the group calls Guru Swami. The coconut has its water drained through a small hole, and ghee is poured in while everyone chants. Then the coconut is sealed, the offerings are placed in the front pocket, the provisions in the back, and the bundle is tied and lifted onto the pilgrim's head.

Remember: the ghee coconut is filled with prayer, not in a hurry.

5. Why carry it on the head?

Pilgrims carry the irumudi on their heads, and only those who carry it climb the Pathinettam Padi, the 18 sacred steps of Sabarimala. At the shrine, the ghee from the coconut is offered to Ayyappa in the Neyyabhishekam (ghee abhishekam), and the empty coconut shell is offered into the great sacred fire near the temple, the Aazhi.

Remember: first the bundle goes up the hill, then the ghee goes to Ayyappa.

6. The lesson inside the bundle

Families explain the irumudi in many ways. One explanation children like: the front part is for God and the back part is for ourselves, and both are carried together, in balance. Another says the coconut is like our body and the pure ghee inside is like a good heart, offered with love. Either way, the bundle teaches a child that we carry our duties and our devotion side by side, and that a long journey is easier when the load is packed with care.

One small thing to try today

  • Pack a pretend irumudi with a dupatta or a cloth bag: in the front, things you would like to give (a drawing, a flower, a thank-you note); at the back, what you need for a walk (water, a snack).
  • Play the song together and ask your child to tap the beat on the bundle.

FAQ

What is inside an Ayyappa irumudi?

The front part (munmudi) holds offerings, above all a coconut filled with ghee, plus items for worship. The back part (pinmudi) holds the pilgrim's own food and needs for the journey.

Who can carry the irumudi?

A pilgrim who has kept the vratham carries it, after the Kettu Nira ceremony. Only pilgrims carrying an irumudi climb the 18 sacred steps.

What happens to the ghee coconut?

At Sabarimala the ghee is offered to Ayyappa in the Neyyabhishekam, and the coconut shell is offered into the Aazhi, the sacred fire near the temple.
